#+title: Denden example: a small blog

A small real site built with Denden: a home page, an about page, a blog with tags, and a few posts that show what Org gives you (code, callouts, a table, footnotes, a figure). The site is one =denden-define-site= form in =example-site.el=, plus one output of its own. Denden's default layout and page give the markup; =static/style.css= gives the layout, in the spirit of [[https://bearblog.dev/][Bear Blog]]; and the colours, of the page and of its code, come from Emacs's own Modus Vivendi theme.

Live demo: https://denden.idlip.in/

* Files

- =example-site.el= :: the site definition, and =example-site-modus-css-output=, which writes =modus.css= from the Modus Vivendi palette (=modus-themes-get-color-value=). The theme is loaded but never enabled, so a build does not change your Emacs.
- =static/style.css= :: the layout. Files in =static/= are copied to the site as they are.
- =content/= :: the pages. =_index.org= is the home page, =about.org= a plain page, and =blog/= holds the posts. =blog/_index.org= is the blog page; Denden lists the posts under it, newest first.

* Building

From the =denden= folder: =just build= writes the site to =example/public/=, =just web= serves it at http://localhost:7777, and =just deploy= publishes it to the repository's =gh-pages= branch, which GitHub Pages serves at https://denden.idlip.in/ (the domain is in =static/CNAME=). From Emacs, load =example-site.el= and run =M-x denden-build=.

To change the look, edit =static/style.css=, choose other Modus colours in =example-site-colors= and =example-site-code-colors= (or another Modus theme), list other style sheets in =:stylesheets=, or give the site a =:page-shell= of your own.
